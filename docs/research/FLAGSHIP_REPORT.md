# Flagship study — Do raw performance metrics misrepresent project-manager contribution?

_Generated by `python scripts/run_flagship.py`. Do not edit by hand; re-run the script._

**Data:** synthetic organisation; 298 project managers with settled milestones. seed: 20261013 · analytics: 0.1.0 · data fingerprint: 3079f287fd35 · bootstrap resamples: 200

**Ground rules.** Selection rules use observable data only and were fixed before results were seen. No person is designated as superior in advance; findings follow the data and the validated method. Adjusted estimates are associations conditional on measured context — not causal effects of a person.

## Step 1 — Raw delivery outcomes
Raw on-time rates range from 0% (10th percentile) to 78% (90th). The measured conditions of each person's projects alone predict 46% of the differences in their raw schedule variance — before anything about the person is considered.

## Step 2 — Assignment differences
Assignment is not random: project complexity clusters by project manager (ICC 0.34; Kruskal–Wallis p = 7e-10), and people with harder portfolios have lower raw on-time rates (Spearman -0.39). Raw rates therefore partly measure the portfolio, not the person.

## Step 4 — Uncertainty and unsupported conclusions
A raw dashboard would single out 59 people as top performers and 60 as weak. Once conditions and uncertainty are accounted for, the evidence supports 24% of the 'top' verdicts and 22% of the 'weak' ones. Typical uncertainty is ±7 pp; 15 people get no conclusion at all.

## Does it hold on real data?

- **EBSPM (488 real projects, 3 organisations).** Across 488 real projects from 3 organisations, project size and measured context account for 23% of the variation in cost per function point, team indicators for 5% and the organisation itself for 6%. Adjusting for size and context leaves the order of organisations unchanged, but the gaps widen (adjusted gaps are 1.3× the raw ones): raw figures can understate, not only overstate, differences. Projects flagged as having a fixed, experienced team cost 47% less per function point on raw figures, but 9% less once size, context and organisation are accounted for (95% -38% to +32%) — raw team comparisons overstate the team effect. A context model trained on two organisations does not beat a simple median when applied to the third.
- **Desharnais (77 real projects).** In 77 Canadian projects, each extra year of manager experience is associated with +6.4% hours per function point after adjustment (95% -1.2% to +14.7%). The interval includes zero: years of experience alone do not show a clear link to productivity — a reason not to treat tenure as a proxy for contribution.

## Limits
- Synthetic organisation: results show the method's behaviour where the truth is known, not real-world accuracy.
- Adjusted estimates are associations conditional on measured context; unmeasured context remains.
- Public real-world data supports the project- and organisation-level claims only; no public dataset can validate individual estimates. See [PUBLIC_DATA_VALIDATION](PUBLIC_DATA_VALIDATION.md) and [MODEL_CARD](MODEL_CARD.md).
